Betterlink 0.2.0 replaces the gimbal D-pad with a virtual joystick and makes
the two axis speeds independently adjustable, and it puts your favorite
presets on the viewfinder itself.

## A joystick for the gimbal

Direction and speed in one gesture. Drag the puck out of the well and the head
follows: shove it to swing across the room, ease it out to creep the last few
degrees. The pad could only say "that way, at whatever the slider is set to";
the joystick says "that way, this fast", and how fast is how far you pushed.

Let go and the puck snaps home and the head stops. So does leaving the
Dashboard, and so does switching control style mid-drag — a gimbal left
driving is the worst thing a control like this can do, so every way out of the
gesture stops it.

There is a deadzone around the center, so a stray click near the middle will
not creep the head, and an axis whose share of the push rounds to nothing
stops rather than crawling — a near-vertical shove pans no sideways drift.

**The D-pad is still there.** A segmented control in the bar switches between
them, and the pad is unchanged: for a single precise nudge, a button is easier
to aim than a stick.

## Pan and tilt speeds, separately

Two sliders instead of one, with a link toggle that ties them together if you
liked the old single-speed behavior. They set a *ceiling*: a full joystick
deflection reaches it, and anything less is a proportion of it. The same
settings live in the new Gimbal section in Settings, bound to the same
preferences, so the control bar and the settings pane are one control in two
places rather than two that can disagree.

Speeds are measured, not guessed: pan runs to 65°/s and tilt to 41°/s at the
top of their ranges, and the response is linear, so half a push really is half
the speed.

## Favorites on the viewfinder

Star any preset in the preset pane and it appears in a row above the controls
on the Dashboard, one click from the shot. Presets you use once a month stay
in the pane; the two or three you actually switch between all day are on the
screen you are already looking at.

## Known issue

The camera latches a movement command internally, so if Betterlink is force
quit or crashes while you are mid-drag, the head keeps moving until it reaches
the end of its travel. Releasing the puck, leaving the Dashboard or switching
control style all stop it normally — this only affects an abnormal exit.
